Inbound Interest

Kraken reached out proactively. They currently use Hypernative and Blockade for on-chain monitoring but are evaluating new tooling. Michael Be made clear they’re “always looking for the latest and greatest security tooling” and acknowledged “nothing’s perfect.”

02 What TestMachine Presented

The presentation focused on the Token Custody product — AI-powered adversarial testing that simulates contracts to produce risk profiles.

  • Proactive vs reactive: Asks “is this token inherently dangerous?” not “is something happening right now?”
  • EVM simulation via Alloy/revm: Forks chain, executes transactions, watches state changes
  • Bayesian risk scoring: Hundreds of factors, trained on hundreds of thousands of tokens
  • LLM-free: No hallucination risk, deterministic, fast
  • Coinbase integration: Live for 1+ year, covers centralized exchange and DEX listings
  • 100% retrospective rug-pull detection rate
  • Network analysis: Graph-based deployer/funder network detection, behavioral primitives

03 What Kraken Cares About

This is the most strategically important section. Michael Be revealed several key priorities that map directly to Kraken’s operational pain points.

Auditability (IPO-Driven)

“As we explore a potential IPO, one of the big pain points for us is auditability.”

They need historical state data (not just current snapshots), evidence lockers, and point-in-time risk assessments they can show auditors. Current tools don’t always provide this. This is their #1 pain point and the strongest lever for TestMachine.

Role-Based Access & Audit Logs

Michal asked about rogue employee scenarios — can someone silently disable webhooks? They need RBAC and complete audit trails for any tool touching money.

“We get special scrutiny around who did what.”

Custom Risk Thresholds

They want configurable alerts — when risk crosses their acceptable threshold, trigger a review process. Human-in-the-loop, not automated actions.

“I don’t want to have to rely on vibes.”

Private Threat Intelligence Integration

Kraken has internal fraud flags, Telegram monitoring data, and suspected fraud clusters that aren’t public. They want to feed private flagged addresses and labels into TestMachine’s classification engine. Tenant-specific, not shared publicly.

Multi-Stakeholder Needs

Security team, fraud team, listing team, threat intelligence team — all use similar data differently. Any tool needs to serve multiple internal consumers with different views and access levels.

Strategic Insight

The IPO angle creates genuine urgency. Kraken isn’t evaluating tools casually — they need audit-ready tooling now. Every pain point they revealed maps to a feature TestMachine either has or can build quickly. This is a rare alignment of timing, need, and capability.

06 Competitive Positioning

Kraken already uses two on-chain monitoring tools:

  • Hypernative — Monitoring, supports automated actions
  • Blockade — On-chain monitoring

TestMachine Differentiation That Landed

  • Proactive code analysis vs reactive monitoring
  • LLM-free = no hallucination, deterministic, auditable
  • Coinbase at scale as proof point
  • Bayesian network approach with real data (not “a bunch of if statements”)

“A lot of vendors do promise that they have machine learning... in our experience under the hood, sometimes that’s basically just a big bunch of if statements.” — Michael Be

Positioning

Frame TestMachine as complementary to Hypernative, not a replacement. Proactive (TestMachine) + reactive (Hypernative) = complete coverage. This reduces internal friction from teams already invested in existing tools.

07 Deal Assessment

Buying Signals

  • Inbound: Kraken reached out to TestMachine (not the other way around)
  • Full engagement: 60-minute meeting ran full duration with deep technical questions
  • Senior attendance: 3 technical team members including Director of Security Testing
  • Internal champion signal: Michael said “this is super interesting” and committed to socializing internally
  • Integration questions: Asked detailed how-it-works questions, not surface-level
  • IPO disclosure: Revealed IPO pain points — high trust signal
  • Extensibility interest: Asked about customization for their specific use cases

Risks

  • Stakeholder alignment: Multiple internal teams (security, fraud, listings) need to agree
  • Incumbent tools: Already have Hypernative and Blockade — this is additive, not replacement
  • Feature gaps: Several features need building (historical API, RBAC UI, audit logs)
  • EVM-only: Limitation noted, though Kraken is primarily EVM
Overall Assessment

Strong first meeting. Kraken has real pain points that align with TestMachine’s capabilities. The IPO angle creates urgency — they need audit-ready tooling NOW. The follow-up technical workshop is the next gate. Probability of advancing to pilot: high, contingent on addressing the identified gaps.
